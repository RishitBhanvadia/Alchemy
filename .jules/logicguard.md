## 2024-05-08 - Fixed Incorrect Normalization Logic
**Bug:** The `normalise` function in `server/controllers/resultController.js` incorrectly forced the values of `na`, `nb`, `ni`, and `nc` to sum to exactly 100 by calculating `nc = 100 - na - nb - ni`. This led to a "silent" logic bug when `c` was 0 but the sum of rounded `na`, `nb`, `ni` was less than 100 (e.g. `a=33, b=33, i=33, c=0` becomes `na=33, nb=33, ni=33, nc=1`). The artificial non-zero `nc` could corrupt logic that checks for exact absences or presences of chemical `c`.
**Root Cause:** Rounding individual percentages dynamically can cause their sum to slightly deviate from 100 (e.g. 99 or 101). The previous author tried to "fix" this mathematical artifact by dumping the remainder into `nc`, without verifying if `c` was actually present.
**Learning:** When normalizing independent variables into percentages, calculate each explicitly using `Math.round((value / total) * 100)` rather than subtracting the sum of the others from 100. If an exact sum of 100 is strictly required, the remainder should be distributed using a Largest Remainder Method, but for simple threshold checks, independent rounding is safer and preserves zeroes.
